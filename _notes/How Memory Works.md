**Memory is basically a collection nerve cells firing in a specific pattern.** When we experience something new, the neurons fire. The information is sent to the hippocampus to be decided into disposal (which becomes [[Sensory Memory]] lasting for fraction of a second), retain for a short amount of time (which becomes [[Short-Term Memory]]) or file it into [[Long-Term Memory]] (a collection of memories in our brains when it is decided that it can be used for gaining experience or avoiding past mistakes). 

These memories can be strengthen by repetition, when the the same collection of neurons are fired over and over again, strengthening the bond. Memories are stored primarily in these places-

- [[**Hippocampus:**]]  These store episodic memories- the events that happens to us (birthdays, dinners, meetings, etc)  and are stored for later access. Without a hippocampus, one cannot retain events beyond few minutes, as proved with the Case of Henry Molaison, whose brain was removed to cure his epilepsy.
- [[**Neocortex:**]] Here lies information from certain memories. These can be general knowledge, events we learn from the news, information from your doctor or someone's favourite colour. These occur as not events but as facts. These are theorised to form when we sleep.
- [[**Amygdala:**]] This attached emotional significance to memories. It helps in increasing the strength of retention of the memory. Hence, memories with strong emotions, like love, grief, ecstasy or terror are clearer to us than ones of boredom. Amygdala also plays a significant role in forming memories, especially ones associated with fear. Fearful memories are mostly formed after few repetition. Research on the amygdala is relevant as it is relevant to the way brain processes trauma and stress, and causes PTSD or C-PTSD.

These are aid to the formation of Explicit Memories, which are required to be consciously recalled. 

- [[**Basal Ganglia:**]] This aids in the formation of motor memories and habits, i e, procedural memory. This is the kind of memory that does not get affected by Alzheimer's. However it can be impaired with disorders like Parkinson's disease.
- [[**Cerebellum:**]] This also aids in similar functions, but while basal ganglia links a situation to an action (e.g.- driving to work in the same route without thinking about it and multitasking), cerebellum coordinates the actions to ensure smooth movements (e.g.- stepping out of the car without falling on your face).

These are aid to formation of Implicit Memories, which are not required to be recalled. They are ones that we do not lose, like swimming.

- [[**Prefrontal Cortex:**]] This helps in retaining short-term, working memory that is stored for a few seconds, like remember the last line of a conversation so we can respond to it in accordance.. The space for these memories are short, as too many information at once might not be processed and stored.

*Memories are not always accurate.*

As proved by Elizabeth F. Loftus in her experiments one can instil a wholly new memory merely by verbal reinforcement. Like in the [[Shopping Mall experiment]], when she manged to convince several people, that they were lost in a mall as children as were rescued by an old lady.

Even altering words can change how one recalls a memory. As proved, when the subjects to whom the car crash was narrated as two cars smashing into each other, recalled shattered glass, while the ones to whom it was narrated as two cars hitting each other, recalled none. 

Even changing articles can alter ones memory. In [[Eyewitness Cases]] it was found using 'the', instead of 'a' resulted in more affirmative responses. (E. g.- Asking "Did you see *the* blue car?", instead of "Did you see *a* blue car?" will result in more affirmative responses.)

The brain itself can impair its memory, hiding memories particularly traumatic events to protect itself from the negative emotions invoked from the event (-a common side effect [[PTSD and C-PTSD]], when a lot of gaps in a traumatic memory or period is present. These are often called repressed memories.)

Hence, memories are highly unreliable as they are prone to modification.
